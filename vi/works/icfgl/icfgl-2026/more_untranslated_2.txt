Second, the regret of consent: the wound experienced by users, mainly women, who agreed to urban renewal under asymmetric information, funded rebuilding through taxes, and were displaced outside structures their consent enabled. April 2024 women-trader protests at Rumuwoji Mile One Market, led by Princess Wariso against Rivers State Government over shop reallocation, exemplify this; broader market-women displacement literature documents the pattern (Adama, 2025; Adeoye, 2013; FIWON, 2016). Architects’ renderings, peer-reviewed in the Journal of the Nigerian Institute of Architects (Nigerian Institute of Architects, 2024), function as morally injurious instruments when pitching leaders toward foreseeable displacement, in the structural sense Dekker et al. (2022) describe for Boeing 737 MAX disasters. Anchored in Crenshaw’s (1991) intersectionality, Fineman’s (2008) vulnerability theory, and Mohapatra and Wiley’s (2019) relational autonomy, the paper drafts an eight-element feminist legislative architecture: domestication of ILO Convention No. 190 (ILO, 2019); statutory recognition of moral injury covering workers, families, place, and displaced users; mandatory women-led safety and ethics committees; vulnerability-responsive licensing; relational-autonomy informed consent; statutory Place Injury Impact Assessment; an Indigenous Landscape and Footpath Reserve Rule; and an Anti-Gentrification Displacement Standard with rights of first refusal at protected prices. Operationalisation is proposed through a chartered profession of Moral Injury Auditor, predominantly women-led, trained at the intersection of law, finance, insurance, psychology, architecture, ecology, and AI data science. Agentic AI revolution dissolves the coding gatekeeper as the 2025–2050 urban build-out opens sector-scale demand at US$151.1 trillion cumulative (PwC, 2026), positioning women to lead this profession.
